# Hidrofoil — brief

**Spec:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 24 fps, 30.0 s, 120 BPM, composed score (music only), Turkish captions. Style: math (manim look), π hero.
**Idea:** A boat speeds up; lift grows with v²; when L = W the hull leaves the water and drag drops.
**Device:** the green dot (L) climbs the parabola; when it touches the red W line the boat takes off.
**Format:** F2 chronology, morph chain (proven): 5 eras, 3 shape morphs (7.0, 12.0, 17.5), 1 hard cut on the payoff (23.5).
**Constant:** the counter `L/W` at the top of every frame, computed from one tracker q = v/v₁ (L/W = q² until take-off, then 1.00).
**Colour code:** L green · W red · v blue · D orange · ρ teal · S purple · C_L gold · yellow only for L = W.

## Claims and sources
- Lift equation L = ½ρv²SC_L (standard; first estimate only). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foil_(fluid_mechanics), https://aerofoils.de/blogs/blog/how-does-a-hydrofoil-work
- Water is ~800× denser than air (≈1000 kg/m³ vs ≈1.2 kg/m³ at sea level). Same sources; ratio computed (≈830, shown as "≈ 800" / "~800").
- A foil pushes water down and is pushed up; once lift carries the weight the hull rises clear and drag drops (wetted area shrinks); lift then balances weight. Sources: https://ffden-2.phys.uaf.edu/211_fall2002.web.dir/Abraham_Tsigonis/page2.htm, https://www.boatingmag.com/story/boats/using-a-hydrofoil-to-improve-efficiency/
- "Speed ×2 → lift ×4" follows from L ∝ v² (the equation itself).
- No specific speeds, weights or drag numbers are shown; drag arrow lengths are illustrative.
